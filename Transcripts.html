Make your own course transcript

Build clean, numbered transcripts from your Peterson Academy enrollment — then use them with AI and study tools. We don’t host copies of PA transcripts here.

Why we don’t share transcripts here

Peterson Academy owns its course materials — lectures, videos, and the transcript text that appears inside the platform. Their terms give students a license to use the academy, not to republish that content elsewhere. So this site does not host or redistribute PA transcripts, videos, quiz banks, or other direct copies.

What we do share is the study craft: how to turn your enrollment into clean, numbered transcripts you can use with AI and study tools — and, on the rest of the site, original overviews, podcasts, and guides built from courses we’ve paid for. That’s the university habit: learn the material, then produce your own work product. It isn’t a mirror of Peterson Academy, and it isn’t a for-profit resale of their catalog.

If you don’t have access yet, enroll at Peterson Academy (your own subscription). Then follow the steps below.

1. Hand-copy from Peterson Academy into Google Docs

Open the course and its lectures on petersonacademy.com with your own enrollment.

For each lecture, copy the on-screen transcript text into a Google Doc. One doc per course is fine.

Keep lecture titles as headings. Don’t rewrite the words — just get the text across accurately.

When you’re done, use File → Download → Microsoft Word (.docx) so you have a file to attach in the next step.

2. Clean & number with the skill

In Cursor (or a similar agent tool), install or paste the skill from clean-course-transcript-skill.md.

Attach the .docx (or paste the Google Doc export) and run the skill.

You’ll get a file named like [Original] - Numbered Paragraphs.docx, with lecture-scoped paragraph numbers (1.1, 1.2, …).

3. Optional — PDF

Want a printable PDF? Use numbered-transcripts-to-pdf-skill.md with LibreOffice headless conversion on the numbered .docx. Don’t rebuild the PDF from scratch — convert the Word file so numbering and layout stay in sync.

These are the same skills Publius Nakamoto uses to prepare transcripts for overview videos, study guides, and the rest of the course pipeline.
